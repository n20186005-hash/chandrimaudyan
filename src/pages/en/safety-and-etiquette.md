While spending time at Chandrima Udyan (চন্দ্রিমা উদ্যান), some safety rules and etiquette should be followed. The park is in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, Dhaka, close to the National Parliament building and Crescent Lake, and at its centre is a mausoleum complex. So more rules may apply here than at an ordinary walking spot. This page is an independent guide, not official; always verify the current rules locally.

## Time of day and after dusk

For safety, it is best to visit in daylight. After dusk, with less light, fewer people and a situation that is harder to assess, extra caution is needed. Especially if you are alone, a woman, or with children, plan to return in daylight and arrange your transport in advance. For daylight and crowds, see [opening hours and schedule](/en/opening-hours/).

## Etiquette at the mausoleum and mosque

At the centre of the park there are a mausoleum complex, a mosque and a memorial room. At such religious and memorial sites, it is good to stay calm, dress appropriately and follow the authorities' rules.

- Remove your shoes in the indicated place before entering the mosque.
- Avoid loud singing, whistling or shouting.
- Show respect to other visitors at religious and memorial sites.

### Practical advice on clothing

For the mausoleum and mosque areas, choosing modest and comfortable clothing is good — such as knee-length clothing and something to cover the shoulders. There is no need to worry about very tight or bright clothing, but showing respect is the main point. On hot days, loose cotton clothing is more comfortable, and in the monsoon a spare set to change wet clothes is helpful. For the mausoleum, mosque and etiquette, see [the mausoleum, mosque and etiquette](/en/memorial-complex/).

## Rules on taking photos

Check the signs or restrictions before taking photos. Photos may be prohibited somewhere, especially in security-sensitive places. If photographing other people or the crowd, be courteous and seek consent. Using a drone or professional equipment may need separate permission, so find out in advance.

### Crowd photos and privacy

When photographing in a crowd, some people may feel uncomfortable, especially women or children. So if someone's face clearly comes into the frame, seek permission first or check before publishing the photo. Before publishing photos of families and religious sites, it is good to consider the feelings of those concerned, and do not use others' photos for your own convenience.

## Security checks and bags

Being close to the National Parliament area, there may be checkpoints or bag searches. Cooperate, it is better not to bring prohibited items, and keeping large bags to a minimum is convenient. To ensure your own and your family's safety, the authorities' instructions are final.

### What is better not to carry

To save time at checkpoints, it is better not to carry unnecessary large bags, sharp objects or suspicious items. Whether these are prohibited cannot be stated with certainty, so leaving them out is the safe choice. If you have essential medicines or documents, keep them easy to show.

## Advice for women and solo travellers

If you are a woman or travelling alone, plan to go in daylight and return before dark. Even when crowded, choose relatively open and well-lit paths, and step aside if a stranger crowds you for no reason. Keep your phone charged, arrange transport in advance, and let someone know when you reach your destination. If possible, travelling with a companion is safer.

## Safety with families and children

If you go with children, keep a safe distance by the lake and keep reminding them not to lean over the wall. Writing your phone number on a child's hand or keeping an identification note helps. If a child slips away in the crowd, stay still, inform the security staff, and agree on a meeting point in advance. For the full detail with children, see the [family and children guide](/en/family-guide/).

## General etiquette

| Situation | Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moving in a crowd | Queue up, be patient | Pushing, arguing |
| Litter | Put it in a designated bin | Dropping it on the ground |
| By the lake | Stand at a safe distance | Leaning over the wall |
| Trees and flowers | Look from a distance | Breaking, tearing |
| Other visitors | Show respect | Loud singing, annoyance |

## In short

Go in daylight, be careful in the evening; observe etiquette at the mausoleum and mosque; check restrictions before taking photos, and cooperate at security checks. Verify the current rules locally.
