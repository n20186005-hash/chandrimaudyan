Dhanmondi is a busy residential area of Dhaka, and getting from here to Chandrima Udyan means crossing from one area to another. It is usually this cross-area journey that takes the most time, because you have to pass through stretches like Kalabagan and Farmgate, where traffic pressure stays almost all day. So the two main questions when travelling from Dhanmondi are — which route to take, and what time of day to set out. This is an independent guide; check the current situation before you go.

## Who travels on this route

- Those coming from Dhanmondi, the Dhanmondi residential area or nearby areas
- Those travelling without a car and wanting to keep costs down
- Small families wanting to go out around midday
- Those wanting to combine the last stretch with the metro or a bus

With large luggage or older companions, it is better to cut the changes between areas and take a ride-share directly.

## Possible routes from Dhanmondi

Heading out of Dhanmondi towards the parliament building and Manik Mia Avenue brings you close to Crescent Lake and Chandrima Udyan. Set the navigation destination as “Chandrima Udyan, Crescent Lake Road” or “Chandrima Udyan”. Specific roads, turns and crossings can change through the day, so —

> Exact walking distances and safe crossings change through the day; check locally which route is convenient before you go.

### Via Satmasjid Road and Kalabagan

One of the more-used ways out of Dhanmondi is along Satmasjid Road towards Kalabagan and Farmgate; this is the route with the most traffic pressure at busy times. Another possible direction is to go round by Dhanmondi Lake and enter the parliament side along Mirpur Road. Each route's condition changes from day to day, so check the current traffic on your navigation before setting out.

## Which vehicle to choose

| Vehicle | Best suited to | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ride-share | To save time, with family or luggage | A fixed drop-off point near the parliament area |
| CNG | Medium distance | Agree the fare first |
| Bus | Low cost | Slow at busy times, routes change |
| Rickshaw | The short final stretch | Slow at busy junctions |
| Metro connection | To avoid traffic | You must first reach a station |

## The reality of crossings and traffic

On the way out of Dhanmondi there is traffic pressure at several junctions; on rainy days conditions slow further. When crossing a major road, use the footpath, a footbridge where available, or a signal, and allow time. At busy times a rickshaw ride will feel long; in that case a CNG or ride-share may be quicker.

### Beside Dhanmondi Lake and crossings

On the inner roads of Dhanmondi there are many rickshaws, so it is easy to find one nearby. But the road beside Dhanmondi Lake has narrow stretches for walking, and the junction carries a strong flow of cars; so to cross a major road, find a signal or footbridge. On rainy days roads get slippery and slow, when two-wheeled vehicles need extra caution.

## Getting off and planning the return

After getting off the vehicle, head towards the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/) and Crescent Lake to the park's entrance. There may be security checkpoints around the parliament and Military Museum area, so follow the instructions and get off only at an official point. On the return you may hit the same cross-area traffic, so —

- Choose the return time to avoid the peak as well.
- Be more careful after dark and arrange your transport in advance.
- It helps to arrive in daylight and plan to head back before dark.

### Think about alternative routes on the return

Returning the same way may put you in the Kalabagan–Farmgate traffic again, so either choose a slightly different time or check the navigation for an alternative route. Once it is dark, the lanes inside Dhanmondi can be dimly lit, so keep to the main road and arrange your ride-share in advance.

## Luggage and limited mobility

With large luggage it is hard to manage the crowd on a bus or rickshaw, so a ride-share or licensed taxi is more comfortable. If your mobility is limited or you use a wheelchair, plan to cut the walking after getting off and be dropped as close to the entrance as possible, and see the [separate guide](/en/accessibility/) on entrance facilities.

### Luggage on a rickshaw or bus

A Dhanmondi rickshaw has little room for a large bag, and luggage is hard to manage in a crowded bus; so with heavy bags a ride-share or licensed taxi is better. If your mobility is limited, cut the walking after getting off and be dropped right by the entrance, and allow time for waiting if there is a checkpoint.

## Combining the metro from Dhanmondi

There is no metro station directly in Dhanmondi; but on entering the parliament-side area you can get on at MRT Line 6's [Bijoy Sarani](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) or [Agargaon](/en/metro-agargaon/) station to combine the journey. Doing so avoids a large part of the cross-area traffic.

### When this helps

- When you can reach an MRT station a short distance from Dhanmondi.
- When you want to avoid the evening traffic on the return.
- When travelling without a car and wanting to keep costs down.

### Check it in advance

Which station and connection is convenient for your starting point, and the current service times, should be checked in DMTCL's information.

## Walking or rickshaw if you are nearby

Even from the very near part of Dhanmondi, walking the whole way is not realistic because the distance is long; so it is better to combine the last stretch with a rickshaw or CNG. Setting out around midday means less sun and comparatively less traffic.

- Avoid the morning office rush and the busy evening hours.
- On rainy days allow more time for the journey.
- With children or older companions, keep the walking at both ends short.

## In short

The main challenge of the journey from Dhanmondi is the cross-area traffic. Head towards the parliament and Manik Mia Avenue, travel outside the peak times, and to save time choose a ride-share or CNG — or to cut cost, a bus and rickshaw.
