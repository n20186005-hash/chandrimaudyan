Many visitors to Chandrima Udyan want to see, right next door, the most familiar structure in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar — the National Parliament building (Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban). It is both an important government institution of the country and, in its exterior form, a focus of interest for lovers of architecture. Below is what can be enjoyed from outside, what limits there may be for visitors, and what is important to check before you go.

## What is known about the building

- The building was designed by the renowned architect **Louis I. Kahn**. It grew out of the plan to create a modern administrative centre in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar (source: beautifulbangladesh.gov.bd).
- It stands in **Sher-e-Bangla Nagar**, right beside Chandrima Udyan and Crescent Lake. So it can easily be combined with a visit to the park.
- It is a **working government institution**, not a museum or an entertainment venue — it is important to keep this difference in mind.

## The administrative centre of Sher-e-Bangla Nagar

It is easier to understand the parliament building if you see it not as a single structure but as part of a planned district of the city. In Louis I. Kahn's plan, administration, housing and water bodies in this area were all thought of together; [Crescent Lake](/en/crescent-lake/) was part of that plan too. Walking from the park gives some sense of this arranged environment.

## Viewing it from outside, from the park

> This is a working government area. There may be a security perimeter, checkpoints and off-limits zones. If you are stopped at any boundary, do not argue — follow the instructions.

- **The shore of the park and Crescent Lake:** from the side you get the exterior form of the building and its reflection in the water — available along with the park visit itself.
- **Towards Manik Mia Avenue:** from the wide road you can see the distant view and the overall layout of the area.
- **Photography:** it may be assumed that photographs can be taken from public open spaces, but keep a safe distance; do not carry a camera into off-limits areas.

### Leaving the park: which way to walk

Leave by the notice and signboard at the park's main entrance, then head along the road alongside or along [Manik Mia Avenue](/en/nearby/manik-mia-avenue/). Traffic on the main road moves fast, so look both ways carefully before crossing and use a footbridge or a marked crossing. Because this adjoins the parliament area, if there are checkpoints or barricades, use the route the security staff direct.

## What visitors should do and the limits

- Whether you can go inside, join a guided visit or attend any event — all of this should be **verified with the authorities**; the current rules can be found on the website or by phone.
- During government office hours there is more activity and security; movement may be restricted on special days.
- It is proper not to get involved in any political or administrative dispute and simply to enjoy the architecture and the surroundings.

### What to verify before you go

- Ask the relevant authority in advance about entry permission, rules and timings.
- Movement may be restricted because of public holidays, special days or security arrangements.
- Be certain in advance about the rules on photography and about off-limits areas.

### What to do if it is closed or restricted

There is no need to be disappointed if entry is limited or closed — for many visitors, enjoying the exterior from the shore of the park and Crescent Lake is the main attraction. Afterwards you can walk along Manik Mia Avenue, or head towards the Bangladesh Military Museum or Novo Theatre.

## Planning together with the park

The simplest plan is to finish the walk and the viewing spots in the park and then look at the exterior of the parliament area from the side. After that you can head, if you wish, towards the [Military Museum](/en/nearby/military-museum/) or [Novo Theatre](/en/nearby/novo-theatre/). Bijoy Sarani station on MRT Line 6 is used as the nearby option; check the timings with DMTCL.

## Day time, crowds and the plan for returning

### What time of day is good to go

Viewing and photography are generally more comfortable in daylight. During government office hours there is more activity and security, so it is convenient to avoid the morning crowd and the late-afternoon peak. Plan to finish the outside viewing and return before dark.

### Afterwards, back to the metro or the park

After viewing from outside you can head towards Bijoy Sarani station on MRT Line 6; Agargaon station is also an option. If you want to return to the park, go back the same way and follow the safety instructions. Do not assume a fixed walking distance or time — it changes with traffic and the conditions of the day; check the timings with DMTCL.

## Rules and etiquette when moving around the parliament area

The parliament building is a working government institution, so it is important to observe some basic courtesy and rules while moving around here.

- Follow the security staff's instructions and the barricades; do not try to enter off-limits areas.
- Do not obstruct crowds of visitors or government work; stay in public open spaces.
- Do not get drawn into any political or administrative discussion; keep to the main purpose of enjoying the architecture and surroundings.
- Do not assume that a specific ticket or permission is needed to view from outside; but if there is any rule, verify it.

## Who it suits: planning by visitor type

This area is not the same for everyone — the shape of your day depends on what you are looking for.

### For those interested in architecture and history

If you are drawn to Louis I. Kahn's plan and the building's exterior form, the main pleasure is a slow walk that takes in the park's shaded paths, the lake shore and the wide road alongside. There is no need to hurry; you can move along looking at the building's form from a few different angles.

### With family and children

If you come with children, stay in the public open spaces and respect the security boundaries. There is no need to keep them standing in one place for long — showing them the exterior and then moving on to the park or a nearby museum makes it easier to hold their interest.

## Preparation and what to carry

### Clothing and weather

Prepare for Dhaka's sun, dust and sudden rain. Light-coloured comfortable clothing and a hat help, and in the monsoon an umbrella or raincoat is useful.

### Handy things to keep

- An identity document (may be needed if there is a security check)
- A water bottle and a light snack
- A fully charged phone and a power bank
- Some cash in hand, in case it is needed

> No specific entry fee, permission or opening time is assumed here; verify with the relevant authority or an official source before you go.

## In short

The parliament building is a working government institution — be certain before going inside, otherwise enjoy the architecture from outside. Walking through the park, Crescent Lake and the wide road alongside gives a sense of Louis I. Kahn's plan. Before you go, always verify the rules, timings and permissions.
