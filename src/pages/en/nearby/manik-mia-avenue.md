The wide road that runs past Chandrima Udyan is the one known as Manik Mia Avenue. Many people visiting the park pay attention only to the inside, yet walking along both sides of this road makes it easy to read the planned layout of Sher-e-Bangla Nagar and the exterior of the parliament area alongside. Below is what to see here, where to take photographs and what to be careful about.

## Why the road is well known

- **Wide and planned:** because it adjoins Chandrima Udyan, the road is broad and there is more room to walk on both sides.
- **Open sightlines:** from parts of the road you can see the exterior form of the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/) and the nearby structures.
- **A link in the area:** it is notable as a junction connecting the park, the parliament area and the route towards the [Military Museum](/en/nearby/military-museum/).

## The city mood and setting

Manik Mia Avenue is not just a road — it is part of the administrative and civic setting of the area. In the morning there is a light crowd, during the day more vehicles, and towards evening a different atmosphere. Choosing your time with Dhaka's traffic, dust or wet roads in mind makes for a better experience.

## Photo spots and sightlines

> Vehicles move fast. Standing in the middle of the road to take a photo or view the scene is dangerous — use the safe walking strip at the side.

- **The open stretch beside the park:** catches the mix of the outer layout and greenery with the city.
- **The footpath beside the road:** more usable for distant views and architecture.
- **In the evening light:** the city looks different under the night lights; but be more careful of crowds and vehicles in the dark.

### Stepping onto the road from the park and crossing

You can start walking along Manik Mia Avenue right from the entrance beside the park. The road is wide, but vehicles move fast, so look both ways before stepping out and use the walking strip. Which side to leave from to find the nearest crossing can be checked on the signboard at the entrance and in local guidance.

## Time of visit and cautions

- **Vehicles:** look both ways carefully before crossing the road; use a designated crossing.
- **Security controls:** because it adjoins a government area, there may be barricades or checkpoints on special days.
- **Daylight:** the best time to walk and take photos is usually daylight; plan to return before dark.
- **Season:** November–February is comfortable; in the monsoon a sudden shower can make the path slippery.

## Planning together with the park

The easiest thing is to finish the walking path inside the park and then take in the area alongside along Manik Mia Avenue. After that you can head, if you wish, towards the exterior of the parliament area, the Military Museum or [Novo Theatre](/en/nearby/novo-theatre/). Check the timings of Bijoy Sarani station on MRT Line 6 with DMTCL.

### What to verify before you go

- If movement is restricted or there is a checkpoint on a particular day, find this out from the relevant source in advance.
- Dhaka's weather and traffic change through the day; pick your time before setting out.
- Be sure about the rules on photography and about off-limits boundaries.

### What to do in rain or at busy times

On wet roads walking can be slippery, so carry an umbrella and take care in the monsoon. If the pressure of vehicles is high during office hours, stay on the walking strip beside the road and avoid taking photos in the crowd. When the afternoon rush eases, walking is comparatively more comfortable.

## Day time, crowds and the plan for returning

### What time of day is good to go

In the morning a light crowd, during the day more vehicles and in the evening a different atmosphere — this is how the avenue's face changes. Daylight is the safest time to walk and take photos; plan to return before dark.

### Afterwards, back to the metro or the park

After walking along the road you can head towards [Bijoy Sarani station](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) on MRT Line 6. If you want to return to the park, go back the same way. Do not assume a fixed walking distance — it changes with traffic and the state of the path; check the timings with DMTCL.

## Safety, rules and etiquette

- Do not step suddenly into the road in front of vehicles; use footbridges and zebra crossings.
- Because it adjoins a government area, follow the security staff's instructions and the barricades.
- Put litter in the designated bins; keep the park and the road clean.
- Keep bags and valuables with you in a crowd.

## Who it suits: who wants what

This road is not just a route to pass through — walking, taking photographs or understanding the layout of the city, each purpose uses it in a different way.

### If you love walking

A slow walk along the wide footpath brings together the park, the broad road and the arranged setting of the government area. Starting in daylight and finishing before dark is the most comfortable.

### If you want to take photographs

The open stretch adjoining the park and the walking strip beside the road give good distant views. Never stand in the middle of the road; choose a safe position and take your photos away from the crowd.

## Walking safely and choosing a time

### When the crowds and traffic are lighter

A light crowd in the morning, more vehicles during the day and a different atmosphere in the evening — this is how the avenue's face changes. Avoiding office hours and choosing a quieter time makes both walking and photography easier.

### In the monsoon and the heat

In the monsoon the path can be slippery, so keep an umbrella or raincoat and take care; in the heat carry water and a hat. Sudden rain makes travel take longer, so allow extra time for the return.

## In short

Manik Mia Avenue is an easy addition to a park visit — from both sides of the wide road you can see the layout of the city and distant views of the parliament area. Take photos from a safe position, walk in daylight and follow the rules of the government area.
