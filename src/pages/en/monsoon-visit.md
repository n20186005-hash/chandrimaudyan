Visiting Chandrima Udyan (চন্দ্রিমা উদ্যান) in the monsoon has its own challenges — sudden rain, slippery paths and mud. But the season also has advantages — there are fewer crowds and the greenery looks even more vivid. This page is an independent, third-party guide; rather than making any certain claim about the rain or about closures, it sets out how to plan for the monsoon.

## Monsoon weather

In this region the monsoon is taken to run from June to September. During this time the sky stays cloudy and there is occasional heavy rain; but it may not be continuous — sometimes the sun returns within a few hours. Temperatures are relatively lower, but it feels more humid. Checking the day's forecast before you go makes planning easier.

### What the rain is like by month

Generally the rain begins in early June-July and is heaviest in July-August; it tends to ease towards September. But there is variation from year to year, so it is better to pick your day based on the forecast. Sudden rain is more likely on cloudy days, so it is safest to always carry an umbrella or raincoat.

### Air and temperature during the rain

In the monsoon the air is more humid, so it feels muggy and you sweat more. Light, breathable clothing is more comfortable. After rain the temperature drops a little, but it can feel hot again once the sun returns — so layering your clothing is a good idea.

## What changes in the park in the rain

| Situation | What happens | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light rain | You can keep walking | Walk slowly with an umbrella/raincoat |
| Heavy rain | Slippery, muddy, quickly soaked | Wait under a shelter |
| Thunderstorm | Being outdoors is risky | Take shelter in a safe cover or car |
| Waterlogged paths | Movement can be difficult | Postpone the visit |

## What to bring

- A light, folding umbrella or raincoat.
- Non-slip shoes; grippy shoes are better than open sandals.
- A plastic bag for wet clothes or socks.
- Water and dry snacks.
- A power bank, as phone battery can drain faster on rainy days.

### How to protect your phone and bag

Getting your phone or camera wet in the rain can damage it, so a waterproof pouch or zip-lock bag helps. Avoid taking your phone out with wet hands, and if you need to check a map in the rain, do so while standing under a shelter. Keeping dry clothes separately inside a wet bag keeps the rest of your things safe.

### Shoe and clothing preparation

Grippy shoes that dry quickly are good; heavy or smooth-soled shoes are risky in the monsoon. A light raincoat may be more convenient than an umbrella, because it leaves both hands free. A dry towel with you helps if you get soaked.

## Backup plan

If there is a lot of rain, consider an indoor place nearby instead of the park — such as a museum or a science and technology centre. But verify their opening hours and tickets separately; they may be closed during the monsoon or on holidays. Once the situation settles, you can come back to the park and look around. Among the indoor options, the [National Museum of Science and Technology](/en/nearby/science-museum/) is nearby.

## Safety

- Do not stand under trees or in open fields during thunderstorms.
- Do not walk quickly on wet ground — there is a risk of falling on slippery paths.
- Be extra careful on wet walls or stairs by the lake.

### Rules for walking on slippery paths

On wet paths, walk slowly in small steps, and hold on to something if there is a railing. Be especially careful on stairs or slopes, and avoid moving quickly or running. If you have children or older companions, hold their hands and avoid wet grass or muddy areas.

### What to do in a thunderstorm or sudden squall

If a thunderstorm begins, avoid open fields, under trees or high ground and take shelter in a safe cover or a car. Stay away from metal poles, railings or flagpoles, and use your phone less while wet. As soon as the storm rises, pause your visit and resume once the situation settles. For the general safety rules, see [safety and etiquette](/en/safety-and-etiquette/).

## The park's condition after rain

After continuous rain, paths can become waterlogged and the ground muddy, especially near the lake. After two or three hours of rain, it is worth checking what the area is like before walking. After rain, trees and plants stay wet and slippery, so on shaded paths watch your footing.

## Tips for photographing in the monsoon

A cloudy sky softens the light, so the greens look deeper and gentler in photos — a big advantage of the monsoon. But rain drops on a camera or phone can leave marks, so wipe them repeatedly and keep a waterproof arrangement. Dry your hands before taking out your phone while wet, and on dark cloudy days using a little more exposure or HDR gives better results.

## Who enjoys the monsoon most

For those who love taking photos in a quiet setting away from crowds, the monsoon is well suited — a cloudy sky softens the light and the green trees look deeper. But if you have children or older companions, keep the visit short in view of the slippery paths. With children or older companions, see the [family and children guide](/en/family-guide/).

## In short

In the monsoon, keep the chance of rain in mind and carry an umbrella, raincoat and grippy shoes. In light rain, continue your visit; in heavy rain or thunderstorms, take safe shelter; and keep an indoor place in mind as a backup.
