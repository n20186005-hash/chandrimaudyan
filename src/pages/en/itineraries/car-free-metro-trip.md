Traffic in Dhaka takes a lot of time out of travelling by car. So if you want to see Chandrima Udyan in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar without your own car, MRT Line 6 is your simplest aid. Getting off at a station and then a short walk or a short rickshaw or CNG ride — this combination cuts both the time and the hassle. The route below is arranged for the car-free traveller, who does not have to fight the traffic.

## Who it suits, where it starts and where it ends

This route is for travellers without a car, students, solo visitors and those who want to avoid the traffic. It starts when you get off at an MRT station and ends by returning the same way after touring the park and its surroundings. It is also convenient for students or travellers on a limited budget.

- **Start point:** [Bijoy Sarani](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) (or Agargaon) station on MRT Line 6.
- **End point:** returning to the same station.
- **Suits:** car-free travellers, students, solo visitors.

### Who this route suits less

If you would rather travel comfortably by car or ride-share, or you have very heavy luggage and small children or older companions, a fully car-free route can be somewhat tiring. In that case, get off at the station and take a rickshaw or CNG directly to cut the walking. On heavy-rain or very crowded days this route moves even more slowly — allow extra time then.

## The walking order, step by step

1. Board MRT Line 6 and get off at Bijoy Sarani (or Agargaon) station.
2. Leave the station by the permitted exit and walk the short distance, or take a rickshaw or CNG.
3. Enter the park gate and go around the lakeside walking path.
4. If needed, view the outside of the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/); rules for going inside are separate.
5. When your time is up, return to the station and take the same line back.

### Which station to get off at — a comparison

Bijoy Sarani station is generally treated as the closest station to the park, so it is convenient if you want to cut the walking. [Agargaon station](/en/metro-agargaon/) is a little further, but rickshaws and CNGs are readily available. Which station is better for you depends on your starting point and the time; do not assume a specific distance, but check with DMTCL.

## Transport and meals

For car-free travel the mainstay is the metro; the last stretch is by rickshaw, CNG or ride-share. Which gate is convenient after leaving the station can change during the day, so check the signage before you exit. There may be shops nearby for food; do not assume a particular shop or price — check on arrival. It is good to carry water while walking.

### Metro cards and preparing for the journey

Before entering the station, find out how to buy a ticket or card, and allow a little time for the bag scan. At busy times the ticket counter queue can be long, so setting out a little earlier is better. Even if there is room to stand inside the train, move carefully in a crowd and mind the steps when entering and exiting.

## Opening days, tickets and restrictions

The metro's service times, the condition of the stations and any possible rules all change — check the latest information from DMTCL's official sources. The park's and the parliament-side area's opening times and ticket information also change from time to time; no specific time or fee is confirmed here. Read the notice at the gate on arrival.

> Outside the station the route may change because of roads, barricades and security checkpoints; third-party map estimates of walking distances are not always accurate.

### Avoiding the crowd on the return trip

On the return trip in the evening, the metro gets crowded around the time offices empty; so returning a little earlier or a little later means less pressure. If you cannot find a seat on the return journey, move slowly and hold a handrail. Deciding in advance where you will exit the station reduces hesitation in a crowd.

## Safety when travelling alone

When travelling alone, it is better not to keep a camera or phone openly exposed and to keep your bag safe in a crowd. Keep emergency numbers and let someone know your return time. If someone behaves uncomfortably, go to a guard or staff member; follow the security staff's instructions. Returning before dusk is safer than walking alone at night.

## Ways for students to cut costs

The metro fare is generally lower than a ride-share; so make the metro your mainstay for travel. If you have companions, sharing a rickshaw or CNG lowers the cost per person — agree the fare first. Instead of buying outside, bringing your own water and snacks saves money. Do not assume any particular discount; check with DMTCL.

## If you want to drop something

If time is short, just walk in the park and head back; skip the parliament forecourt. If the metro is packed at a busy time, take a slightly later train. If it rains, cut the walking portion and spend time in an indoor place.

## In short

A car-free trip means the metro plus a short walk or rickshaw ride — this mix is simple and traffic-free. The key point: check the station connections and service times in advance, and do not take the walking distance for granted.
