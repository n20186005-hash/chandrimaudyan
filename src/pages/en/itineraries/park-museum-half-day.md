If you come to Dhaka's Sher-e-Bangla Nagar and have a pull towards history and culture, you can fit both Chandrima Udyan and the [Bangladesh Military Museum](/en/nearby/military-museum/) into the same day. There is room to walk a little more slowly here, with plenty to see along the way. This half-day route is arranged so that you can see both places without rushing. But the museum's opening rules are different, so keep that in mind from the start.

## Who it suits, where it starts and where it ends

This route is for visitors interested in history, culture and military heritage, including students, and anyone who likes to wander slowly. Starting at the park in the morning leaves more time in hand. It starts at the park entrance and ends on the way back after the museum. If you have older companions, cut the walking portion shorter.

- **Start point:** the entrance of Chandrima Udyan (Sher-e-Bangla Nagar).
- **End point:** the Military Museum (the area beside Bijoy Sarani).
- **Suits:** lovers of history and culture, families and students.

### What to do if the museum is closed

The Military Museum works to its own rules — it normally closes one day a week and opens after midday on Fridays. If you find the museum closed, do not cancel the whole plan; instead extend the walking part of the park. Spending time on the shores of [Crescent Lake](/en/crescent-lake/) and near the bridge, and viewing the outside of the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/), makes the day worthwhile. As an alternative, if a nearby science-themed institution is open, check its closing day too in advance.

## The walking order, step by step

1. In the morning, enter the park, walk slowly along the lakeside path and take in the surroundings.
2. Leaving the park, pass through the parliament-side area and head towards the Military Museum.
3. Look around the museum's outer forecourt; ask at the counter about the entry rules and timings.
4. Inside, view the galleries and exhibitions in the order of the day; allow time for each part as you go.
5. If you are tired, use the food facilities inside the museum grounds, then set off on the way back.

### From the park to the museum — route and distance

The two places are very close to each other, so a short rickshaw or CNG ride is convenient; if you are willing to walk, you can go on foot too. I am not assuming a specific distance or time here, because barricades, security checks and traffic change the route. If you use the metro, [Bijoy Sarani station](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) is nearby; getting off there, walking in the park and then going on to the museum is also easy.

### How much time to allow at each part

On a half-day route, allowing about 45–60 minutes in the park and about 90–120 minutes in the museum usually keeps the rush down. Walking pace and interest vary from person to person, so change this split to suit your own rhythm. If photos or exhibits take longer in the museum, shorten the park portion, or you can do the reverse.

## Transport and meals

The park and the museum are close, but traffic can still take time. MRT Line 6's Bijoy Sarani station is nearby, so the metro plus a short rickshaw or CNG ride is convenient. You can also go by ride-share. Regarding food, some facilities are said to exist in the museum grounds, but do not assume a particular shop or price — check the opening times and timings on the spot. It is good to carry light snacks and water.

## Opening days, tickets and restrictions

For both Chandrima Udyan and the Military Museum, the closing days, timings and ticket rules are separate and changeable. According to the museum's own website it normally closes one day a week and opens after midday on Fridays; but timings can change with the season or on special days. So do not plan around a fixed day — check the official website or the ticket counter before you go.

> There is no certain large car park for visitors inside the park; the notice at the entrance can change quickly. Third-party map estimates of walking distances or times are not always accurate.

### How to check tickets and entry rules

It is best to check the entry fee, closing days and timings before you go — look at the official website or ask at the counter. There may be rules inside about photography, bags or food; find these out before entering. Rather than trusting prices or times printed on a third-party banner, give priority to official sources.

## What to know before entering the museum

Before entering, ask at the counter about that day's opening times, tickets and the rules about bags. You can also ask whether there is any discount or special arrangement for students or group visits, but do not assume one. If there is a visiting order for the galleries or any off-limits area, follow the announcements and instructions. An exhibition may be closed for repairs, so it is good to find out in advance.

## If you have children or older companions

If you have children or older companions, it is better not to spend too long inside the museum and to extend the park portion instead, since it is easier to rest in the open air. The condition of the walking paths and seating inside the museum cannot be stated with certainty in advance, so ask on arrival. Keeping water, light snacks and a hat for the children is convenient.

## If you want to drop something

If time is short, keep the park walking portion brief and give the museum more time; or you can do the reverse. If the museum is closed, simply stop at the park and the parliament forecourt nearby. If it rains, choose a place where you can spend longer indoors.

## In short

A morning walk in the park, then the Military Museum — these two steps fill half a day. There is one main caution: check the closing day and timings yourself, because the museum's rules differ from an ordinary place to visit.
