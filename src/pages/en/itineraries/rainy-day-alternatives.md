In Dhaka's [monsoon season](/en/monsoon-visit/) a plan does not always hold — sometimes the sky is clear, sometimes the rain pours down suddenly. If rain falls on the day you are going to Chandrima Udyan, there is no need to cancel the whole trip; just change the plan a little. This alternative route keeps a balance between open walking and indoor places, so that the day is still worthwhile even if rain comes. A realistic scheme for a rainy day is given below.

## Who it suits, where it starts and where it ends

This route is mainly for families, students and group visitors who come to Dhaka in the monsoon (June–September) — whose plans depend on the weather. It starts at the park entrance, but if the rain increases you move quickly to a nearby indoor institution; it ends at that institution or a safe shelter. Do not assume any particular place to be "the indoor alternative".

- **Start point:** the entrance of Chandrima Udyan (Sher-e-Bangla Nagar).
- **End point:** a nearby indoor institution or a safe shelter.
- **Suits:** monsoon visitors, families and students.

### Deciding by the kind of rain

If a light drizzle continues, a short walk with an umbrella is possible, but if it is pouring with thunder, leave the open area and take shelter indoors at once. Decide by watching how dark the sky is and how the wind is building. If you have a weather forecast, it is good to compare it, but do not rely on an exact time.

## The walking order, step by step

1. If the rain is light, first walk briefly in the park; keep an umbrella with you.
2. If the clouds thicken or the rain increases, leave the open part and come under cover.
3. Choose a nearby indoor institution — a museum, theatre or [cinema](/en/entertainment/military-museum-cinema/).
4. Ask at the counter about the entry rules and timings, then go in and spend some time.
5. When the rain stops, set off on the way back; move slowly on wet roads.

### Safety when walking on wet paths

On wet footpaths, slippery steps and standing water, feet can slip, so move slowly and wear non-slip shoes. Avoid electric wires or open drains and do not walk on wet grass. Wet paths become even more unreliable in the evening, so try to return while there is light.

## Transport and meals

Traffic usually increases on rainy days, so using [Bijoy Sarani](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) or Agargaon station on MRT Line 6 cuts the walking portion. The last stretch is by rickshaw, CNG or ride-share. For food, find out on arrival whether the indoor institution has facilities; do not assume a particular shop or price. It is good to carry water and dry snacks.

### Equipment to stay dry

Besides an umbrella, a light raincoat helps, because it keeps both hands free and you do not have to fend off an umbrella in a crowd. It is good to keep a plastic cover or waterproof bag for your phone and camera. A small towel or handkerchief comes in useful if you get wet, and carrying a spare set of clothes for children is convenient.

## Opening days, tickets and restrictions

Whichever institution you choose — museum, theatre or cinema — their closing days and timings and ticket rules are separate and changeable. On a rainy day there may be crowds or a special closure. No specific time or fee is confirmed here; check at the counter or the official website on arrival. In the case of the [Military Museum](/en/nearby/military-museum/), it is normally said to close one day a week and open after midday on Fridays — check it.

> In thunder or heavy rain, avoid open fields, standing under trees or walking on wet stairs. Safety on the move comes first, sightseeing after.

### What to check on a rainy day

On a rainy day an institution may be temporarily closed or open late, so call or check online before you go. Even if the crowds are smaller, wet floors and umbrella rules can add time. Ticket rules or show times may change, so do not take them for granted.

## What to do in thunder or heavy rain

In thunder, do not stand under trees, in open fields or near tall structures; go to a nearby solid building or a designated shelter. In heavy rain, drop the walking entirely and choose an indoor institution directly. Taking children and older companions to a safe place first is the priority. If water accumulates, movement may be limited, so wait patiently.

## Plan for returning if the rain does not stop

If the rain does not stop, rather than waiting a long time, take a ride-share or CNG to a nearby shelter and wait there, or stand somewhere safe. Plan your return assuming the traffic will take longer. Avoid a long journey in wet clothes and sit somewhere dry if possible; if you feel unwell, it is better to head home promptly.

## If you want to drop something

In heavy rain, drop the park walking entirely and go straight to an indoor institution. If you have children or older companions, be extra careful. If the rain does not stop, it is wise to return safely rather than wait any longer.

## In short

Monsoon rain does not mean the trip is cancelled — choosing an indoor place instead of open walking makes the day worthwhile. The key point: change the plan according to the weather, walk safely, and check the institutions' opening days and rules in advance.
