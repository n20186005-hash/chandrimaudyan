When you go out in Dhaka with children, mixing in a taste of science and discovery instead of only walking around makes the day far more memorable for them. In the Bijoy Sarani and Agargaon area there are several places where children can learn hands-on and by seeing. This one-day route is kept light in that spirit — no rush, but following the children's rhythm. It starts with the park's open setting, so that the children stay free while walking.

## Who it suits, where it starts and where it ends

This route is for families with small children, school and college student groups, and visitors keen on science and technology. Starting at the park in the morning lets the children walk in an open setting, and afterwards you can enter a museum or theatre. It ends on the way back after the last chosen institution. You can run this route without trying to include everything in one day.

- **Start point:** the entrance of Chandrima Udyan (Sher-e-Bangla Nagar).
- **End point:** the chosen museum or theatre (Bijoy Sarani/Agargaon area).
- **Suits:** families with children, student groups, science lovers.

### Which institution suits which age of child

Generally, younger children find a visual experience like [Novo Theatre](/en/nearby/novo-theatre/), or the aircraft at the [Air Force Museum](/en/nearby/air-force-museum/), appealing; older children are more drawn to the hands-on exhibits at the [National Museum of Science and Technology](/en/nearby/science-museum/). Choose one or two by age and interest, not all in one day. What is actually shown varies from institution to institution, so check in advance.

## The walking order, step by step

1. In the morning, enter the park and walk beside the lake; give the children some free room to move about.
2. Leaving the park, choose the closest science-themed institution first.
3. Allow at least one time-block there; if the children get tired, take a break in between.
4. Move on to a second institution — for example the Air Force Museum or the National Museum of Science and Technology.
5. After eating and resting, see the rest if time allows; otherwise stop at the veranda and head back.

### Nearer institution first or farther — choosing the order

When children are tired, choosing the nearer institution first is wise — it wastes less time in travel. Start with whichever is closest in the Agargaon/Bijoy Sarani area; later, if time and energy allow, go to the farther one. Too much walking makes children tired quickly, so include rest breaks in between.

### Splitting time by the child's rhythm

Allowing at least 60–90 minutes at each institution lets the children see it without rushing. Instead of a continuous loop, give water and snack breaks in between, which keeps moods good. If one institution takes longer, shorten the next — do not try to cover everywhere in one day. If a child falls asleep or gets irritable, take a break.

## Transport and meals

The area is close to Bijoy Sarani and [Agargaon stations](/en/metro-agargaon/) on MRT Line 6, so the metro plus a short rickshaw or CNG ride is convenient with children — you stay out of traffic sitting in a car. Ride-shares also work. There may be various shops nearby for food; choosing light and familiar food for children is good. Do not assume a particular shop or price — look on arrival, and carry water and small snacks.

### Planning food and rest for the child

The rules on food and water inside a theatre or museum may differ, so find out before entering. Choose light and familiar food for the children; avoiding spicy or new things is better. If there is a place to wash hands or rest, a short break there keeps children calm. Thinking about the feeding time in advance helps avoid the crush.

## Opening days, tickets and restrictions

Novo Theatre, the National Museum of Science and Technology and the Air Force Museum — each has different closing days, timings and tickets, and these change from time to time. No fixed day or figure is confirmed here; check the relevant institution's official website or counter before you go. There may be some rules inside the museum (for example about luggage, food or photography); find these out on arrival.

> Taking a rest before and after entering a museum with children matters, because a continuous loop tires them. Show or exhibition times change often, so ask at the counter in advance.

## Planning to avoid the children's fatigue

Trying to cover many places in one day with children increases fatigue, so two or three institutions are enough. Start with something light — the open walk in the park — then one institution, food and rest, and finally another. Look out for seating or shade in between. If a child falls asleep or looks irritated, it is better to shorten the plan.

## What to bring along

Keeping water, light snacks, a hat or umbrella, and hand sanitiser and a spare set of clothes for a small child is convenient. On rainy days, spare shoes and a plastic cover for electronics come in useful. Find out what is not allowed inside the museum or theatre before you enter. Writing the children's names and a parent's phone number on a slip of paper is a safe habit.

## If you want to drop something

If the children are tired, dropping the second institution and visiting just one before heading back is sensible. If it is very hot outside, extend the indoor portion at the museum and cut the walking portion. On a rainy day, choose a place that is indoors, such as a theatre or museum.

## In short

An open walk in the park plus a nearby science-themed institution — this mix fills the children's day. The key point: instead of covering everything in one day, pick two or three, and check each place's opening days and tickets in advance.
