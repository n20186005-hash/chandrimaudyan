Chandrima Udyan is a well-known park in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, Dhaka, beside the National Parliament building and on the shore of [Crescent Lake](/en/crescent-lake/). Its present form was not created in a single day — it gradually took its current shape through the mid-twentieth-century city plan, the change in land use and the growth of an administrative centre. What follows is only verifiable background, not a political assessment.

## Sher-e-Bangla Nagar's modern administrative centre

In the 1960s the Sher-e-Bangla Nagar area in the west of Dhaka was planned as a modern administrative centre. The best-known building of that plan is the National Parliament building, designed by the American architect Louis I. Kahn. Official tourism sources state that the parliament building and its surroundings are part of this centre plan.

The scope of the plan was not limited to the building — around it there was an integrated design of water bodies, green grounds and circulation routes, and Crescent Lake and its shoreline grew up as a part of that.

### Where the architecture shows through

Looking at the parliament building, the lake and the open grounds together makes the coordinated idea of the plan easier to grasp. As you walk through the park, watch which direction brings the shape of the parliament building and the curve of the lake into a single frame — that is where the architectural relationship shows. Do not assume that information plaques or interpretive boards will always be present; see for yourself on site what explanation is available.

## From farmland to park

According to the published description of the Dhaka Tourist Police, the land of the present park was previously used for farming and cattle grazing. As urbanisation and the administrative centre spread, this use of the land changed and it gradually turned into a park.

> Different sources may describe the exact timeline and stages of this change differently; to know specific dates, check official or historical sources.

### Reconciling older accounts

Accounts of how the land use changed do not always agree. If you find a different timeline in old maps, parliament-related records or an institution's website, that is not unusual. If you are using this for research or writing, it is better to compare several sources, and safest not to treat any single date as final.

### Which structure is in which part

Generally, the lake and the bridge stretch across the open part of the park, while the mausoleum complex lies towards the centre and the mosque and memorial hall stand close to it. However, security arrangements and maintenance can change the walking routes from day to day. No exact direction or distance for any particular structure is stated with certainty here — follow the notice at the entrance and the signage.

## On the question of naming

The same park is referred to in some places as Chandrima Udyan and in others as Zia Udyan. The word “Chandrima” means moonlight or moonshine; the linguistic explanation of the name and the various English spellings are set out on a [separate page](/en/zia-udyan-name/), so that the main thread of the history remains clear here.

## Walking with an understanding of the history

Knowing this background, the park reads not merely as a place of recreation but also as a chapter in Dhaka's city planning. Seeing the parliament building, the lake and the grounds together makes the twentieth-century planning idea easier to understand. A detailed architecture route is in a [separate guide](/en/itineraries/history-architecture/).

### Steps for a history-minded visit

1. Read the notice and signage at the entrance.
2. Begin walking along the lake shore.
3. Take in the view towards the parliament building from the bridge.
4. Explore the area around the mausoleum complex and the mosque towards the centre.
5. Head back before dark, judging the crowds and the light.

## What to check before you go and on arrival

On a history-minded visit it helps to have one thing in hand — a reliable map link. Using a hand-typed date or a written-out name risks mistakes. On arrival, read the notice at the gate, the signage and the security staff's instructions; if any part is closed for repairs or an event, use the alternative route. This guide does not give any confirmed information on entry fees, opening and closing times or permits — verify these locally.

## History visits by season and time of day

In the dry and cool seasons walking is comparatively comfortable and there is more daylight in which to explore. In the monsoon the paths can be wet and slippery, so walk slowly. In the harsh midday sun, standing for long in the open grounds is uncomfortable; early morning or the late afternoon is better. This guide has no fixed season-by-season schedule or dates for special events — plan using the weather forecast and local notices.

## In short

The history of Chandrima Udyan is bound up mainly with the plan for the modern administrative centre of Sher-e-Bangla Nagar. From farmland it was transformed into a park, and today it is known as a popular green and water-rich area adjoining the parliament building. If you have doubts about specific dates or the stages of the change, check reliable sources.
