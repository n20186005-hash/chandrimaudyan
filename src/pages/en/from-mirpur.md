Mirpur is a densely populated area in the north of Dhaka, and there are two clear ways from here to Chandrima Udyan — one is to take MRT Line 6 part of the way and cover the last stretch by short transport, the other is a direct ride-share along Mirpur Road. Which to choose depends on your time, companions and luggage. Mirpur Road is one of the busiest roads in Dhaka, so the metro is often convenient for avoiding traffic. This is an independent guide; check the current situation.

## Who should travel this route, and when

- Those coming from Mirpur and nearby areas
- Those who want to favour the metro to avoid traffic
- Those travelling with family or in a group
- Those wanting to travel at low cost

With large luggage or a small child, a ride-share may be more comfortable for its door-to-door convenience.

## Going by MRT Line 6

From Mirpur, taking MRT Line 6 towards the stations near Chandrima Udyan is usually the most reliable way, because the railway is not caught in road traffic. [Agargaon](/en/metro-agargaon/) or [Bijoy Sarani](/en/metro-bijoy-sarani/) can be used as the nearest stations; after getting off the station you take the last stretch by rickshaw, CNG or ride-share.

> Check with DMTCL which station is convenient for you and the current service times; exact walking distances change through the day.

### Which Mirpur-side station to get on at

MRT Line 6 runs past Mirpur, so you can start the elevated journey from a nearby station such as Mirpur 10, Mirpur 11, Pallabi, Kazipara or Shewrapara. Decide in advance which station is close to your home or destination, because getting off one station early or late makes a difference to the last stretch.

- At busy times the roads towards the station on Mirpur Road are crowded, so leave home a little early.
- There may be bag scanning or checking, so be ready to open your handbag.
- Before returning in the evening, check the last-train time in DMTCL's information.

## Comparing the two ways

| Point | MRT Line 6 + last stretch | Direct ride-share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effect of traffic | Low, only the last part is hard | High, Mirpur Road is busy |
| Door to door | No, one change is needed | Yes |
| Cost | Usually lower | Can be higher |
| Best suited to | Light bags, those avoiding traffic | Luggage/companions, those wanting comfort |
| Time reliability | Comparatively high | Varies with time and situation |

## Getting off and crossing

After getting off the car or at the station, head towards the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/) and Crescent Lake to the park's entrance. When crossing a major road, use the footpath, a footbridge where available, or a signal. There may be security checkpoints around the parliament and Military Museum area, so follow the instructions and use an official drop-off point.

### Crossing Mirpur Road

Mirpur Road is very busy and wide, so after getting off the station or the ride-share, do not cross suddenly; find a footbridge or signal-controlled crossing. At busy times vehicles are stopped on both sides of the road, so avoid crossing through such gaps. With children or older companions, cross together and hold hands.

## Planning the return

Returning the same way is easiest. However —

- Before returning, check the station's service times and last-train schedule with DMTCL.
- At the busy evening hours the station gets more crowded, so allow extra time.
- Be more careful on journeys after dark and arrange your transport in advance.

### If you need to catch the train back

It helps to use the same station on the return, but the Mirpur-side stations get busier towards evening, so it is better to arrive a little early. Check in DMTCL's schedule when the last train leaves each station. If you are running late, do not wait at the station — keep an alternative plan with licensed transport.

## Luggage and limited mobility

With large luggage, changing from station to station can be hard; in that case a direct ride-share or licensed taxi is better. If you use a wheelchair, plan to cut the walking after getting off, and see the [separate guide](/en/accessibility/) on entrance facilities.

### From Mirpur with family

With family or children, a direct ride-share is often more comfortable than changing from station to station, because getting on and off in the Mirpur Road crowd is hard. If you choose the metro, use the station lift to reduce the hassle of stairs, and let children and older companions step forward first when getting on and off the train.

## Direct ride-share along Mirpur Road

Going directly along Mirpur Road means you travel door to door, so it is comfortable with luggage or companions. But Mirpur Road is one of the busiest roads in Dhaka, so the journey can be long at peak times.

### Navigation and the drop-off point

Set the app destination as “Chandrima Udyan, Crescent Lake Road” so the driver can enter the parliament-side area. Because of security rules the car may have to stop at a fixed point, so on arrival use only the official point the driver indicates.

### When a direct car is better

- With large luggage or a small child.
- When travelling at dawn or late at night, when the roads are comparatively empty.
- When you do not want to change between CNG or bus.

## Things to know before returning from Mirpur

Travelling back from Mirpur, both road traffic and station crowds can add time, so set out with the return plan ready.

- Check the last-train time in DMTCL's schedule.
- In the rain the roads are wet and slow, so allow extra time.
- After dark, do not wait long alone at the station or on an empty footpath.

## In short

To avoid traffic from Mirpur, taking MRT Line 6 to a nearby station and covering the last stretch by short transport is usually better; for comfort and door-to-door travel, a direct ride-share. Check the station's schedule before you return.
