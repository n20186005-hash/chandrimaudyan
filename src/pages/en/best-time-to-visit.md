When Chandrima Udyan is most comfortable depends on two things: the time of day and the season of the year. What the light and heat are like at each part of the day, and which months have favourable weather — plan with both together and the visit is more comfortable. Rather than claiming specific dates, this page sets out the general weather tendencies.

## By time of day

| Time | Light and atmosphere | Suitability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn | Cool, calm, fewer people | Excellent for walking and photos |
| Morning | Mild sun, comfortable | Good for families and older visitors |
| Midday | Harsh sun, more heat | Better avoided |
| Afternoon | Heat eases a little | Acceptable for walking |
| Evening | Light fades, security needs checking | Be cautious |

### The advantages of going at dawn

In the early morning the air is cool and calm and there are fewer people; it is a good time for walking and quiet photography. Transport is less crowded then, so travel may take less time. But with less light at dawn, watch the path carefully and stay alert for safety. A light shawl or jacket is comfortable at dawn.

## By season

In this part of South Asia it is generally dry and cool from November to February, the most comfortable time to walk outdoors. From March to May the heat and humidity rise, making a midday visit uncomfortable. And from June to September it is the monsoon — a chance of sudden rain and slippery paths.

- **November–February:** comfortable and dry; the best time of year.
- **March–May:** hot; go at dawn or towards dusk.
- **June–September:** monsoon; keep an umbrella and raincoat.

### Planning for a monsoon day

In the monsoon the sky may be cloudy without continuous rain; sometimes the sun returns within a few hours. So check the forecast that morning and visit in a gap in the light rain. If heavy rain or thunder is likely, wait in safe shelter. Be careful on wet paths and keep an indoor alternative in mind. For the full detail, see the [monsoon visit guide](/en/monsoon-visit/).

## For light and photography

At dawn and towards the afternoon the light is soft, so photos of people and trees look good. In the harsh midday sun photos look washed out and shadows are hard. If you want good light, plan for dawn or the afternoon, and check the weather as well. For routes built around light, see the [walking and photo route](/en/walking-photo-route/).

### Afternoon light and atmosphere

Towards the afternoon the heat eases a little and the light becomes soft, which is comfortable for walking and photos. At this time shadows lengthen, giving photos depth. But the afternoon can be busier, so if you want fewer people, arriving a little earlier is better. Planning to return before dusk keeps both the light and safety convenient.

## Avoiding the crowds

Weekends and public holidays can be busier. If you want a quiet atmosphere, the early part of the week or the early morning is convenient. But rather than relying on an uncertain idea of holiday and event timings, it is better simply to keep the habit of going early. For the schedule itself, see [opening hours and schedule](/en/opening-hours/).

### Crowds and light together

If you want both a quiet atmosphere and good light, it is better to choose dawn or the morning; then the crowds are fewer and the light is soft. In the afternoon the light is good but it can be busier, so to take photos it helps to go a little before the afternoon. On holidays or special days, when it is busier, planning to go early helps most.

## The effect of special events and holidays

On national days, events or VIP visits, crowds and security rules may increase in the park and the surrounding area. At such times ordinary visitors' movement may be restricted, so it is better to find out in advance. We cannot say for certain what will happen on a specific day — verify locally.

## Checking the weather

Check the forecast the day before or on the morning of the visit, so that rain or unbearable heat can be avoided. Temperature, chance of rain and wind speed — all three affect the plan. Comparing more than one source reduces the chance of error.

### How to read a weather forecast

Look at the chance of rain, the wind speed and the temperature together to judge whether the day is suitable for being outdoors. A morning forecast is not final for the whole day, because conditions change quickly in the monsoon. Comparing more than one weather source reduces the chance of error, and if thunder is likely, put the visit off.

## What to carry by season

In the hot months, water, a cap, sunglasses and light cotton clothes all help. In the monsoon, take an umbrella or raincoat, shoes with good grip, and a plastic bag for wet clothes. In the dry, cool months a light shawl or jacket is comfortable morning and evening. Whatever the season, carrying water and a light snack helps.

## Who should choose which time

For those who love walking or want quiet, dawn or the morning is best. If you want to take photos, choose the soft light of dawn or the afternoon. With children or older companions, it is convenient to go in the morning, avoiding heat and crowds. Avoid travelling alone after dusk without checking the situation.

## In short

Within the day, dawn and the afternoon are comfortable — avoid midday; across the year November to February is best, and in the monsoon be prepared for rain. Check that day's weather before you go.
