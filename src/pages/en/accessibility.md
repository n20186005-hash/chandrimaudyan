## Walking distance and rest

Chandrima Udyan is extensive; it is hard to say exactly how much walking is needed from one point to another. For older visitors or those with limited mobility, a slow pace and frequent rests are the most realistic plan. Do not set a big distance as a target at the start; walk a little first, extend if it feels comfortable, and sit down at once if you feel tired.

### Preparation for wheelchair users

Before going with a wheelchair, learn locally about how smooth the paths are, the slopes and the entrance. If possible, take a helper with you who can assist on uneven ground. Keeping the visit short and resting in the shade now and then is comfortable. Before you get out, ask the driver to stop as close to the gate as possible.

## From transport to the park

How easy the route is from a metro station, a bus stop or a drop-off point to the park also affects accessibility. If someone can help, take them along; rickshaws or short car rides can be used when needed. Before getting out, ask the driver to stop a little closer. For the routes and how to get there, see [how to get to Chandrima Udyan](/en/how-to-get-there/).

### The advantage of having a helper

Having a helper with you makes steps, crowds and uneven ground easier to get through. If needed, fix in advance when and for how long you will need help. In a crowd, a helper holding your hand increases safety. If you travel alone, it is best to go in daylight and decide the return plan in advance.

## Advice for older visitors

- Go at the comfortable times of day (morning or afternoon), avoiding the midday sun.
- Carry water, necessary medicine and a light mat or sheet.
- Avoid unnecessary heavy bags.
- Having a loved one or helper with you helps with steps and crowds.
- Set out with time for rest on the way back.

### How to divide the rest time

For older visitors or those with limited mobility, walking in short stretches — a little walking, then sitting in the shade — is most comfortable. Drinking water at every rest and not letting fatigue build up is good. Keeping a rest spot for the return also reduces fatigue. If you are not sure about seating, carry a light mat or sheet.

## Contact and advance information

The best way to know whether there are specific arrangements for disabled or older visitors is to ask the relevant source in advance. When asking, be specific — is there a ramp, is a wheelchair available for hire, where is the accessible toilet. Comparing two or three sources increases the chance of accurate information.

### What to ask specifically by phone

When asking by phone or in person, do not ask general questions but be specific — is there a ramp at the entrance, is there a path a wheelchair can be pushed along, where is the accessible toilet, and are there benches to sit on. Noting names and contact details helps with checking later. If two or three different sources give the same answer, the information can be treated as more reliable.

> The best thing is to confirm by phone or in person whether the facilities exist; do not rely on online information alone.

## Checklist to verify before you go

Before you go, run through a short checklist — how level the entrance is, whether there is a ramp, whether there are rest benches or shade, and the location of the accessible toilet. Also learn the distance from the drop-off point to the entrance. If the answer to any of these is unclear, assume facilities are limited, and arrange your time and a helper accordingly. For the entrances and map, see [park map and entrances](/en/park-map-entrances/).

## Plan B — if facilities are limited

If facilities are limited, it is safer to keep the visit short, go at the comfortable time of day, and take a helper. If there is no ramp or accessible path, consider spending time in the easily reachable parts outside the park. If needed, choose a nearby place that is easy to reach, and plan to come back another time. For verifying the facilities list, see [visitor facilities](/en/visitor-facilities/).

## In short

The current state of ramps, wheelchairs or accessible toilets could not be verified — so do not take them for granted. Check before you go, take a helper, and travel slowly with a little walking and rest.
