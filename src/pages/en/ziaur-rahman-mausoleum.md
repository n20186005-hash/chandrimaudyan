The mausoleum complex of Ziaur Rahman, at the centre of the park, is one of the main focal points of Chandrima Udyan. Though it is a notable structure, it is essentially a place of remembrance. This page sets out, on the basis of verifiable information, where the complex is, how it is arranged, and how one is expected to behave while visiting. Dress, silence and photography etiquette are covered in more detail in the [memorial and etiquette guide](/en/memorial-complex/).

## Where the mausoleum complex is

The complex stands at the centre of the park, surrounded by the shore of [Crescent Lake](/en/crescent-lake/) and the walking paths. Within the Sher-e-Bangla Nagar area adjoining the [parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/), it is a landmark. From which direction, or through which entrance, you can approach the complex depends on the security arrangements and on day-to-day changes.

> An entrance or a part of the complex may be temporarily closed; check the current situation locally before you go.

### Which direction to approach from

Because the complex is at the centre of the park, you can generally reach it by following the path along the lake shore; however, which direction is open for entry depends on the security arrangements. On arrival, first read the notice at the main entrance, then go along the route indicated. If you need to use an alternative route, it is safest to follow the security staff's instructions.

## Structure and main elements

| Element | Brief description | Note |

| Mosque | Located as part of the complex | Modest dress and silence |

| Green grounds | Open space around the complex | A chance to walk and take in the views |

### Order for walking around

Generally, going entrance → open grounds → around the mausoleum structure → mosque → return path is comfortable when the crowds are light. However, the layout and the walking routes can change over time, so do not treat any order as a fixed rule. Rather than standing for long at any one spot or blocking the way, taking in the view and moving on is the polite approach.

## How to behave while visiting

- Wear modest and courteous clothing; this is a combination of a religious and a memorial setting.
- Avoid loud talk, laughter or noise; keep the atmosphere solemn.
- If you have children with you, ask them to stay calm and treat everyone with respect.
- Put litter in the designated places; keep the grounds clean.

### Practical advice on dress and shoes

Wear modest and comfortable clothing — the walking paths can be long. Shoes that are easy to remove are convenient near the mosque area. A light scarf or shawl with you makes it easier to cover your head if needed. No specific dress code is stated here; follow the notice at the grounds and the local custom.

## Security and entry

Because of the proximity to the parliament area, there may be security checks in parts of the complex and the park. If you are asked for a bag search or given a movement restriction, comply and follow what the security staff say. During a specific conference or a state event, some parts may be temporarily closed.

### What to bring and what not to

It is good to carry water and an umbrella or hat for the sun. Keeping valuables and unnecessary items to a minimum is safer, as there may be searches at a checkpoint. If you bring a large bag or a tripod, find out at the entrance whether there is permission to leave it or to use it. If there are rules about bringing food or drink inside, check the notice at the grounds.

## Cautions about information

- This guide does not give any specific schedule, ticket or contact number for the complex.
- If there is a special event, the crowds and security rules may change; read the notice at the grounds.

### Uncertain points to verify on site

Opening and closing times, permission to enter, rules on photography and any specific contact number are not given with certainty in this guide. During a state event or repair work, some parts may be closed. So, if possible, find out the current situation before you go, and read the notice at the gate on arrival; if in doubt, verify locally or with the relevant source.

## How long it may take

This guide does not give any specific estimate of time, because it varies with the crowds, security checks and your own level of interest. Generally, seeing the complex and the surrounding grounds may take a short to moderate time; more if you want photographs or detailed observation. Waiting in a security-check queue adds further time. Allow extra time in your total for the return journey and traffic.

## Visiting by season and time of day

Going in daylight is most convenient, because the setting tends to be calmer then and the return journey feels safer too. In the monsoon the paths can be wet and slippery, so walk slowly. In the harsh midday sun, standing for long in the open grounds is uncomfortable. On holidays or special days the crowds may be larger, but no specific crowd levels or schedule are given in this guide.

## In short

The mausoleum complex of Ziaur Rahman, at the centre of the park, is a place of remembrance made up of a mausoleum, a mosque and a memorial structure. The main duty when visiting is to observe modesty, silence and security instructions; check any specific times or permissions locally.
