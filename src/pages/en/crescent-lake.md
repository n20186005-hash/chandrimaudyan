Many people think of Crescent Lake as the heart of Chandrima Udyan. This crescent-shaped artificial water body adds to the park's beauty and makes it easy to explore along the lakeside path. This page sets out what the lake is, where it is, how to go around it and what cautions to take.

## What the lake is and where it is

Crescent Lake is a crescent-shaped artificial water body in the Sher-e-Bangla Nagar area adjoining the [National Parliament building](/en/nearby/national-parliament/). The National Parliament building was designed by the architect Louis I. Kahn; according to official tourism sources, the parliament building and its surroundings are part of an integrated centre. The lake creates a balance of water and greenery in the park's setting.

### How the shape of the lake shows itself

Seen from above, the lake is curved like a new crescent, but standing on the ground you do not take in the whole shape at once. Walking along the shore, you can feel the curve of the bank as your direction changes. Stopping now and then to look both ways is an easy way to get a sense of the whole setting.

## Walking the shore and the views

Walking along the path that hugs the shore, the view keeps changing with each curve of the park. As well as looking out over the water, you also notice the city towards the parliament building on the opposite side. The combination of these two directions creates opportunities for photographs, especially near the curve of the lake.

> A part of the walking path may be closed for repairs, or may be slippery in the wet season; check the condition of the path on arrival.

### Walking order and stopping points

A simple flow might be: entrance → lakeside path → stop near [the bridge](/en/crescent-lake-bridge/) → continue along the shore again. You can rest by standing or sitting in a shaded spot along the way, but check on arrival whether there are designated rest areas or benches. If the crowds are heavy, it is better to move on a little rather than stand for long near the bridge.

## Time, light and atmosphere

| Direction / point | General tendency | Advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning light | Soft, little shadow | Comfortable for walking |
| Midday | Strong sun | Carry shade and water |
| Last light of the afternoon | Warm, popular for photos | There may be more crowds |
| Monsoon | Wet, slippery paths | Walk carefully |

These tendencies are based on general experience, not any official schedule. To know the specific conditions, check the weather forecast.

### Light and angles for photography

Using the lakeside path along the curve as a leading line in the frame adds depth to a photo. From the bridge you can shoot in both directions — out over the water, and the other way towards the city and the parliament building. Water creates reflections, so photos tend to look better when the water is calm. No specific light-timing figures are given here; in general, soft light gives the best results.

## Safety and cautions

- Hold children's hands by the water; do not go too close to the edge of the bank.
- After dusk there is less light — stay in well-lit permitted areas.
- Keep valuables safe; be extra careful when it is crowded.
- Put litter in the designated places, so that the water body and grounds stay clean.

### Children by the water

The edge of the bank may have a slope or wet sections, so hold children's hands and stay a little back from the edge. Do not think of going into the water or swimming — this is a place for visitors to walk and take in the views. You can slip on a wet path, so comfortable shoes with good grip are a good idea.

## What to bring for a lake visit

It is convenient to have comfortable shoes, water, an umbrella or hat for the sun, and a phone that is not out of charge. In the monsoon a raincoat or umbrella is essential, because the lakeside path can be wet and slippery. Insect nuisance varies with the season, so if you visit towards evening, take precautions if needed.

## Cautions about the lake's information

Whether there is boating or a lake cruise, what the specific opening and closing times are, or whether any part of the shore is closed for repairs — none of these is given with certainty in this guide. If such facilities or rules exist in the area, they would be shown on the notice at the grounds. So, if possible, find out the current situation before you go, and read the notice on arrival. The park's address, entrances and a reliable map link are in the [map and entrances guide](/en/park-map-entrances/).

## In short

Crescent Lake is the crescent-shaped artificial water body of Chandrima Udyan, where you can walk along the shore and see the view towards the parliament building. The condition of the paths and the safety arrangements change through the day, so take care according to the season and it is better not to go into the water.
