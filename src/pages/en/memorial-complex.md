While walking around Chandrima Udyan you pass various kinds of structures — some connected with remembrance and worship. According to the description by the Dhaka Tourist Police, the park contains a memorial hall and a mosque. This page sets out how to keep up a modest and respectful manner while visiting those memorial structures. General etiquette for a visit is covered in the [safety and etiquette guide](/en/safety-and-etiquette/).

## What is meant by the memorial structures

Some parts of the park are connected with remembrance and worship — such as the memorial hall and the mosque. These are not merely memorial structures; people's reverence and devotion are bound up with them. So visiting this area calls for a slightly different attitude: respect as well as sightseeing.

### What to look at first on reaching the memorial structures

On entering the grounds, first read the notice and signage — where you should remove your shoes, where photography is prohibited, or which path to use for movement is usually stated there. Then set your behaviour according to the setting. If a visitor is at worship, do not disturb them.

## Modesty and behaviour

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Entering the memorial grounds | Wear modest dress, keep silent |
| Entering the mosque | Remove your shoes if needed, leave them in the designated place |
| During worship | Avoid noise, keep movement to a minimum |
| Taking photos | Show restraint; stop if there is a prohibition |
| With children | Hold their hands; running about and rowdiness are not allowed |

## Visiting the mosque

On going to a mosque, keeping modesty in dress and behaviour is ordinary courtesy. During prayer the crowds and movement increase — do not try to push through then, but wait to one side. If someone is at worship, avoid walking in front of them or photographing them.

> The rules for entering different mosques may vary; if there is a specific rule it is usually notified at the grounds — check it there.

### Advice on avoiding prayer times

During prayer the crowds and movement at the mosque increase; if you plan to visit at just that time, it is better to wait a little. If you are not permitted to enter, wait calmly outside. No specific prayer timetable is given here — check locally or look up the mosque's timetable.

## Rules for photography

- Avoid photographing people who are at worship.
- When photographing the memorial structures, check the instructions on the notice or signage.
- Do not use a tripod or large equipment on a busy path.
- If you want to photograph someone, ask permission first.

### Which photos are good and which to avoid

The architecture of the memorial structures or general views of the grounds can be photographed with restraint, but it is better to avoid photographs of worshippers or close-up shots. If a notice prohibits photography, stop at once. Asking permission before photographing a child or a stranger is polite.

## Entry and permission

Visitors can generally go to the memorial structures and the mosque in the park, but there may be restrictions on entry on special days or during worship. If any part is closed, follow the security staff's instructions and check the current situation locally. For the park's layout and entrances, see the [map and entrances guide](/en/park-map-entrances/).

### On special days and events

On a state occasion or a special day there may be temporary rules on entry, photography and movement. No specific dates or list of events are given in this guide — the local notice and the security staff's instructions are what matter then. If it is crowded, move slowly and do not block the path.

## Practical advice on shoes and dress

Choose modest and comfortable clothing; if there is a place to remove shoes near the mosque area, shoes that come off easily are convenient. It is good to have a light scarf or shawl with you to cover your head. Since you may have to carry your shoes by hand somewhere, it is wise to avoid expensive or heavy footwear. No specific dress code is stated here; follow the notice at the grounds and the local custom.

## What is uncertain when visiting the memorial structures

The opening and closing times of the memorial hall and mosque, the specific permission to enter, or a confirmed list of photography prohibitions — none of these is given in this guide. Entry to some parts may be limited during worship or at special events. So, if possible, find out the current situation before you go, and read the notice at the grounds on arrival. To pick a quieter time, see the [best time to visit guide](/en/best-time-to-visit/).

## In short

The memorial hall and mosque in the park are places of remembrance and worship. Modest dress, silence, removing shoes and restraint in photography — these few habits are what make a visit here respectful. Follow the rules on entry and photography according to the notice at the grounds.
